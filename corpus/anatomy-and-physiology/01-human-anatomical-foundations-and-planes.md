# Anatomical Foundations, Spatial Planes, and Directional Terminology

Standard anatomical reference systems form the spatial foundation for vision AI localization,
pose estimation, and structural analysis.

## The Standard Anatomical Position

All human anatomical descriptions, spatial relations, and coordinates assume the subject
stands in the standard anatomical position:
1. Body erect and facing forward (anterior aspect directed at observer).
2. Feet flat on the ground, parallel, with toes pointing anteriorly.
3. Upper limbs positioned alongside the trunk with palms facing anteriorly (supinated) and thumbs pointing laterally.
4. Eyes looking horizontally forward across the transverse horizon.

## Anatomical Planes of Division

Three mutually orthogonal cardinal planes intersect the body:
- **Median / Sagittal Plane**: Vertical anteroposterior plane dividing the body into right and left halves. Parasagittal planes run parallel to the median plane.
- **Coronal / Frontal Plane**: Vertical lateral plane perpendicular to the sagittal plane, dividing the body into anterior (front/ventral) and posterior (back/dorsal) sections.
- **Transverse / Axial / Horizontal Plane**: Horizontal cross-sectional plane dividing the body into superior (upper/cranial) and inferior (lower/caudal) sections.

## Directional Terms

- **Superior (Cranial / Cephalic)**: Toward the head or upper part of a structure.
- **Inferior (Caudal)**: Toward the feet or lower part of a structure.
- **Anterior (Ventral)**: Toward the front of the body or anterior surface.
- **Posterior (Dorsal)**: Toward the back of the body or dorsal surface.
- **Medial**: Toward the midline/median plane of the body.
- **Lateral**: Away from the midline toward the outer perimeter.
- **Proximal**: Closer to the origin of a limb or point of attachment to the trunk.
- **Distal**: Further from the origin of a limb or point of attachment to the trunk.
- **Superficial**: Toward or near the external surface of the body.
- **Deep**: Inward away from the body surface, toward internal visceral structures.

## Primary Regional Divisions

1. **Head (Cephalic)**: Cranium (neurocranium enclosing brain) and face (viscerocranium).
2. **Neck (Cervical)**: Anterior and posterior cervical triangles spanning mandible to thoracic inlet.
3. **Thorax (Pectoral / Chest)**: Enclosed by sternum, ribs 1-12, and thoracic vertebrae T1-T12.
4. **Abdomen (Celiac)**: Anterior abdominal wall, flanks, umbilicus, and 9 regional divisions.
5. **Pelvis & Perineum**: Pelvic girdle connecting axial skeleton to lower limbs, containing internal and external reproductive/urinary systems.
6. **Upper Extremity**: Deltoid shoulder, brachium (arm), cubital (elbow), antebrachium (forearm), carpus (wrist), and manus (hand with digits I-V).
7. **Lower Extremity**: Gluteal/hip, femur (thigh), genu (knee), crus (leg/calf/shin), talocrural (ankle), and pes (foot with digits I-V).
8. **Dorsal Trunk**: Vertebral spine column, scapulae, latissimus dorsi, and gluteal cleft.
